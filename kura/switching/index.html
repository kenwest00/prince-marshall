Switching to KÜRA

Leaving PastPerfect? Take everything with you.

Your catalogue is years of work. KÜRA brings it across, fills in what was never written down, and gives you a record that shows who changed what. Here is how the two compare, from PastPerfect’s own pages.

Six differences worth checking.

We read PastPerfect’s own documentation. Here is what it says, next to what KÜRA does.

Who changed it, and what it said before

PastPerfect Web Edition: Its User History records logins and logouts. The documentation does not describe a log of record edits. Source

KÜRA: Every change is logged: who, what, when and why. Nothing is hard-deleted; records are archived with a reason.

Why it matters: When a value changes or a work cannot be found, you can see who touched the record and what it said before.

One person, one login

PastPerfect Web Edition: Users may share logins, but only one person can be signed in at a time. Source

KÜRA: Every person signs in as themselves, with a role that decides what they can see and change.

Why it matters: A change log is only as good as the login behind it. When a login is shared, the record cannot say who did what.

Getting your records in

PastPerfect Web Edition: Converts data you already have into PastPerfect, at $100 an hour. Source

KÜRA: Our team documents the collection on site, including works not yet catalogued, then keeps the record current.

Why it matters: Conversion moves what is already written down. The gaps in most collections are the works that never made it into the notebook.

Taking your data with you

PastPerfect Web Edition: Catalogue lists export to CSV, or to a KML map file. The guide does not say whether images are included. Source

KÜRA: Open exports at any time, images included. No lock-in.

Why it matters: Your catalogue and its images are yours. A clean way out is part of owning them.

What the basics cost

PastPerfect Web Edition: Full package: $1,245 to set up, then $870 a year for hosting and support. Its pricing page says prices are subject to change without notice. Source

KÜRA: Quoted in writing before you commit, based on your collection, with the cataloguing done for you.

Why it matters: A low entry price still leaves you doing the cataloguing yourself. Compare the whole job, not the licence.

Putting your collection online

PastPerfect Web Edition: A searchable public catalogue is an extra annual fee: $375 a year on the Full package. Source

KÜRA: Publish the works you choose, without ever showing where they are kept or what they are worth. It is part of the catalogue, not a separate site.

Why it matters: Showing your collection to the public should not mean paying twice for it.

Everything you rely on today.

Whatever you use in PastPerfect has a home in KÜRA. You leave nothing behind.

  • Condition reports and care schedules: dated, attached to the work and due when they should be.
  • Location history: home and temporary locations, with who moved what, when and why.
  • Appraisal and insurance details: values with their review dates, and a schedule you can hand to your insurer.
  • Deaccessions: recorded with the reason, and never hard-deleted.
  • Donors, members and pledges: gifts, memberships and the works they relate to, in the same record.
  • Letters, labels and mail merge: thank-you letters, exhibit labels and mailings from your own records.
  • An online collection: the works you choose, published for the public.
